# 06 — Keyword Research & Competitive Analysis

## Core principle
Understand **how and why people search** before choosing keywords. Always keep **searcher intent** front of mind. Great-looking sites fail if content doesn't match what users seek.

## Keyword relevancy, intent, competitiveness
Select keywords on three axes: **relevancy** (to site + business goals), **intent** (matches what the searcher wants), **competitiveness** (can you realistically rank?).
- More specific = less competitive = higher ranking chance + more engaged/converting visitors. (e.g., not "best games" but "best PC games for multiplayer.")

## Stages of search / buyer intent
Query types:
- **Navigational** — looking for a specific site/brand.
- **Informational** — seeking knowledge.
- **Transactional** — ready to act/buy.

Buyer journey (example "Tom buys a computer"): **Awareness** (broad: "best computers to buy") → **Evaluation/Preference** ("best laptop to buy" → "gaming laptop reviews") → **Purchase/Resolution** (exact brand + model). Organic presence helps at all stages but is most valuable **closer to purchase**.

## How users search (Blue Nile Research)
- ~**50/50** split: fragments ("rent textbooks") vs. specific phrases ("rent college textbooks online").
- **27%** phrase queries as **questions** (how, why, what, where). Most common: **how**, then why, where, which, what.
- Use question prefixes to brainstorm long-tail content ideas ("how to find affordable textbooks").
- Consider **seasonality** (Google Trends): launch redesigns / publish seasonal content in the **down season / weeks before** the surge so pages are indexed and ranking in time.

## Head vs. long-tail keywords
- **Head terms** — short, broad, high-volume, very competitive (e.g., "textbooks").
- **Long-tail** — longer, specific, lower volume, **lower competition**, **higher conversion** (later in funnel). They are harder to predict but make up the **majority (~70%)** of all search traffic; head terms are **< 30%**.
- A single long-tail phrase ("online college textbook rental") can target multiple keywords at once and build topical authority that lifts you for more competitive terms over time.
- You don't need to optimize plural vs. singular separately — pages naturally rank for both.

## Keyword research process
1. **Brainstorm + research** a large list (tools: Google Keyword Planner/AdWords, Google Trends, SEMrush, Ahrefs, SpyFu, Keywordspy).
2. **Group keywords into tightly focused themes/buckets** (saves work in mapping; mirrors site sections).
3. **Highlight high-value keywords** by intent match + search volume + competitiveness. Keep unused keywords for future blog/content ideas — don't discard.
4. Validate against your own data: **Search Console** (terms you appear/click for), **Analytics on-site search** (what users search within your site).
5. Watch **semantic relationships** — Google treats "e-textbook" ≈ "electronic textbook"; don't redundantly target both.

## Competitive analysis (the methodology)
Goal: find keywords that align with goals + have enough volume, and judge whether you can realistically rank.
1. **Select candidate keywords** (relevancy/intent/competitiveness).
2. **Identify top competitors** for each term — include **organic competitors** the client may not name (e.g., a news article that ranks). Highlight sites appearing across many terms.
3. **Rank competitors** strongest→weakest by frequency + position across your keyword set (color-code in a spreadsheet).
4. **Evaluate page-level signals** of the top 5 results in a spreadsheet: is the **homepage** ranking or a deep page? title tag optimized? H1 optimized? content quality? resource types (text/video/UGC)? **domain authority / page authority**.
   - Use the **average** DA of the top 5 as a baseline target; note the **lowest** DA in the top 5 as the near-term aim. (Course example: avg DA ~72, lowest ~36.)
   - Most pages rank on **perceived usefulness** (specific dedicated pages), not just the homepage → build topic-specific pages.
5. Set **realistic expectations** with the client based on this analysis.

## Keyword mapping (assign keywords to pages)
- Don't create near-duplicate pages for near-synonym groups (one page each for "e-textbook rentals" AND "electronic textbook rentals" = bad). Map each **theme group** to **one** page.
- **Homepage** = broader scope + slightly more competitive head terms (it holds the most authority).
- Other pages target more specific, less competitive terms (affordability page, comparison page, reviews page, how-it-works page, FAQ from question keywords).
- Before assigning keywords to an existing page, ask: How well does it already rank? Does it match intent? Is user engagement good? Can the keyword fit naturally into copy, title, headings, meta description? Is there low-hanging fruit (a page already ranking for the new term)?
- Deliver as an **Excel/Word keyword map**: page URL, primary keyword, secondary/related keywords, volume, current rank (+ date checked), notes. Prefer editing existing pages (faster authority impact) over new pages where possible.
