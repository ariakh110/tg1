# 10 — Audience & Buyer Personas

## Why personas matter for SEO
A **buyer persona** = a representation of your ideal target user. Without knowing the user, you can't know what they search, how they phrase it, or what words they use. Personas drive **initial keyword research** + content strategy. Build **several** personas to match audience diversity.

## Where to get persona/audience data
**Existing tools you likely have:**
- **Google Analytics** — demographics, interests, other sites visited, devices (desktop vs. phone), top content, click maps, exit pages. Rich starting data.
- **Google Search Console** — search terms your site appears/clicks for; impressions vs. clicks gaps. (Site-performance focused; GA is more user-behavior focused.)

**Direct outreach:** surveys (in-app messages, email past customers, newsletters, onboarding), and internal **support/sales teams** (they know common questions).

**External research tools:** Google Trends (geographic + seasonal interest), SimilarWeb (related topics, traffic sources, topic cloud), Alexa/Quantcast (demographics — less reliable), Followerwonk (Twitter/X follower analysis, geography, gender — only the Twitter slice of your market), Forrester Research, social platforms.

## Building the persona
For initial keyword work, focus on **what they search and how**. A fuller persona adds:
- **Age** — younger users use slang / are tech-savvy; older users may be late adopters (affects keyword choice).
- **Location / regional vocabulary** — "beanie" vs "toboggan," "pop" vs "soda," British vs American ("flat/biscuit/lift").
- **Gender** — may slightly affect vocabulary; avoid stereotypes. (Example: women do most wedding research even for men's tuxedos → also address the bride's queries.)
- **Education level, interests, B2B vs B2C.**
- Give the persona a **name + image** to make them feel human (e.g., "Chuck, a budget-focused college freshman → searches 'e-textbook,' 'rent textbooks online'").

## From persona → keywords
Ask: What problems are they solving? What questions do they ask? What terms/slang fit their age/region? Where do they discover sites (social, referrals)? Are they local? Then write content that speaks to the persona — this naturally incorporates the right keywords and improves UX.

## Persona questions checklist (from the milestone template)
- Gender, age, education level likely?
- What other topics interest them?
- Primary ways they'll discover the site (social, referrals, etc.)?
- Local to a specific area? Does that change their search vocabulary?
- What issues/problems are they trying to solve?
