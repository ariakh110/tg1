# 14 — Client Management & SEO Project Workflow

## Scoping & pricing a project
- Clients first ask "how much will this cost?" Estimate by scoping the effort.
- **Mini audit** to size the project: crawl the site (find hidden sections); review title tags + meta descriptions (optimized? keyword focus? run top keywords through a rank checker; check volume/competition); look for duplicate metadata, missing headings, error pages; review on-page content (amount, optimization, media); quick duplication check (Copyscape / quoted snippet in Google); quick backlink-profile check (spam/over-optimized anchors → past bad SEO / possible penalties).
- Split findings into **quick wins** vs. **complex issues** (deliver value, take time). Set hours/price accordingly. Pricing: hourly (estimated hours) or project-based.
- Site **size + complexity** drives time/cost; for big sites with small budgets, prioritize **top-value pages**.

## Selling & proving value (without giving it all away)
- Frame issues with a **positive spin** + how you'll solve them. Common issues to surface: no keyword direction; over-broad/wrong keywords; keyword stuffing; duplicate content; thin/missing content; content written for engines not users.
- Use a short **PowerPoint** over screen-share; include screenshots, your background, past results, testimonials, findings, expected improvements, process, next steps. Provide some free takeaway value (or they won't sign), but don't give everything.

## Managing expectations
- **Never guarantee #1 rankings** or specific results by a date — anyone who does isn't trustworthy. Google is unpredictable; results depend on competitors + client follow-through.
- Steer the conversation toward **traffic quality/quantity → conversions/sales**, away from specific rankings.
- Timeline: **paid = immediate; SEO ≈ 3 months** to start noticing improvement, then compounds (if best practices maintained). Start SEO **≥3 months before** a busy season; avoid major changes during the busy season.
- Early **keyword fluctuations/spikes** are just signals Google noticed a change — don't over-react; rankings settle over time. Keyword rankings are **one small signal**; real goal = organic traffic + longer-tail variations.

## Kickoff: questions to ask a new client
- Past SEO work / agencies (watch for penalties); reasons for any past **domain changes** (verify proper redirects).
- Are they tracking keyword rankings? (assess attainability + current optimization; beware personalization making their site *look* better to them).
- Most important products/services (customize to their business).
- Industry jargon/slang — do customers use it? (usually avoid slang, but optimize for industry terms where relevant).
- Who implements changes (you, or their dev resource)? How do they measure performance / what goals do they track? Request access to Analytics.
- Prepare a **deliverables timeline** (week-by-week, e.g., 3 months; note meeting points) — kept in a shared doc, updated as you go.

## Quick wins (build early enthusiasm)
Ensure Search Console + Analytics installed; submit XML sitemap; verify robots.txt + blocks; trim keyword-stuffed titles; add text to thin homepage/key pages; add image **alt text**; internal-link to important pages.

## SMART goals
Convert vague marketing goals into **S**pecific, **M**easurable, **A**ttainable, **R**elevant, **T**imely goals. Example evolution: "generate more leads" → "increase leads from our website **through organic search** by **25% per month** by **[date]**." Use existing data for attainable targets; for brand-new sites set conservative, fluid estimates. Realistic timeframe: 3–5 months to align with implementation.

## Reporting
- Run a report **monthly** (often part of a retainer). Educate that keywords fluctuate (algorithm updates, site changes, trends, personalization, localization) — track many metrics, not just rankings.
- Tools: **white-label** reports (keyword + analytics) and/or custom **Excel dashboards** (all data in one place, stored locally, portable). Group keywords into **thematic buckets** (same as research) to spot weak site sections fast.
- Report on **business outcomes** stakeholders care about (conversions/subscribers/revenue), shown visually, not just raw data.

## SEO is ongoing
Not "set and forget." Initial work is front-loaded, but the client must maintain momentum: keep publishing fresh useful content + building off-site relationships/links. Monitor Google for algorithm updates; keep learning; keep practicing.
