# 13 — Metrics, KPIs, ROI & Forecasting

## KPIs (Key Performance Indicators)
KPIs = smaller metrics that show progress toward ROI. SEO takes time, so KPIs demonstrate early traction and manage expectations.
- **Best practices:** know *why* you track each metric (tie to a business goal, or drop it); don't track too many; **benchmark** before SEO so you can compare before/after; compare **like-to-like** — prefer **year-over-year** over month-over-month (accounts for seasonality).

### Common KPIs
- **Organic traffic** — the core KPI. In GA distinguish **users / new users / sessions**; most marketers use **sessions** (best engagement view; a visit). New-user counts can be inflated (cookie blockers).
- **Branded vs. unbranded traffic** — unbranded = true new-audience reach (use Search Console; GA hides keyword-level data). Branded traffic may be driven by other channels, so it's hard to attribute to SEO.
- **Keyword rankings** — useful bird's-eye view but volatile; compare over **longer** periods. With semantic search you get traffic from many related terms you don't track — use the main keyword as a barometer; analyze by site section.
- **Backlinks + site authority** — track total links, **referring domains**, new/lost links, toxic links. Use one consistent tool (Moz/SEMrush). Authority is slow to gain at higher levels.
- **Organic CTR** — Google uses it; higher CTR = more traffic. View in Search Console, filter by page; watch seasonality (holidays raise ad spend → lower organic CTR).
- **Site performance** (speed/Core Web Vitals) — counts toward UX + SEO; part of regular hygiene (SEMrush, Lighthouse).
- **Engagement** (time on site, pages/visit, bounce) — compare organic vs. other traffic.
- **Organic landing pages** — how many pages actually rank high enough to draw visits (different from indexed pages). Situational (a drop can be *good* if it removed unqualified traffic).
- **Goal conversions** — add-to-cart, checkout, wishlist saves, form fills, downloads (set up by client/dev team).

## Benchmarks & tracking
- Capture the site's **pre-SEO state** (use historical Analytics if available); before/after comparisons convince clients.
- Track recommended keyword rankings ASAP. Go back **≥12 months** for traffic to see seasonality (year-over-year view).

## ROI
- **ROI = (Revenue − Cost) / Cost.** Standard formula; adapt as needed.
- SEO ROI is **longer-term** than paid (paid shows immediate ROI but stops when spend stops; SEO compounds and keeps delivering). SEO usually has lower cost-per-lead.
- **Costs to count:** your fees/salary (always include fees if contractor; otherwise follow company model — match what the paid team does), per-article writer costs, link-building agency/article/outreach costs.
- **Attribution problem:** the customer journey isn't linear; organic often assists but doesn't get last-touch credit. Many use **last-touch attribution** → organic is under-credited. Workarounds: count % of organic visits that subscribe/follow and attribute a share of sales; estimate **ad-equivalent value** of earned links (avg DA × industry ad cost); attribute a portion of referral traffic to SEO; relate organic growth to referral growth.
- **Link-building value** is hardest to quantify (not every link is clicked/converts; links build authority/brand). Borrow the **PR team's** valuation model if one exists.

## Forecasting
- **Why:** win buy-in/investment by tying projected gains to monetary value; investors increasingly ask for organic-growth forecasts (most scalable growth).
- **Difficulty:** organic has many uncontrollable variables (algorithm updates, competitors, market, personalization). A forecast is an **estimate, not a guarantee** — it doesn't need to be perfectly accurate.
- **Best practices:** over-communicate your method; explain how you arrived at the estimate; document factors that could change it (e.g., "this depends on Project X shipping by date Y"); keep it **realistic** (investors want big numbers but also credibility); **visualize** simply for busy stakeholders; report on **business outcomes** (subscribers, signups, revenue) not just traffic; pre-empt likely "what-if" questions (what if the deadline slips / we ship early) and build those scenarios in.
