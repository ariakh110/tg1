# 07 — Content Strategy & Content Marketing

## Domain-level content strategy (not just page-level)
Google evaluates sites **holistically**, not only page-by-page. Content organized around a **core theme** builds site-wide topical **authority**.
- Strong **brand** adds legitimacy/trust. (Eric Schmidt: "Brands are how you sort out the cesspool.")
- Build pages dedicated to a central theme, each unique and useful, and **interlink** them with descriptive anchor text (TARDIS → Doctor Who → characters → seasons → episodes). This raises topical authority, engagement, and lowers bounce.
- Blog posts and static pages should link to one another (FAQ short answer → detailed post; blog post → deep product/category page).
- Google can answer a query even when the exact keywords aren't on the page (semantic + entity understanding + brand signals) — so depth and theme matter more than exact-match repetition.

## What makes content "great"
Work backward from the goal. Great content:
1. **Is findable** — right keywords + intent.
2. **Prompts action** — purchase, signup, comment, or at least viewing more pages. Use **calls to action** + internal links.
3. **Is share-worthy** — easy share buttons (above/below/side), "tweet this" snippets, pinnable images for Pinterest audiences.
- Tailor to the audience (seniors: larger fonts, desktop, video; teens: mobile, scannable, catchy subheads).
- Align secondary topics with brand goals (textbook site → "saving money in college," "dorm decor on a budget" → tie back to renting).
- Priority order of topics: closely related first → topics that tie into your offering second → broader audience-interest topics last (avoid confusing visitors).

## Content types by lifetime value
- **Evergreen** — always useful; builds authority + links over years; great for SEO. Avoid dates/current-news/fast-changing tech references; refresh occasionally for freshness.
- **Trending** — leverages **QDF (Query Deserves Freshness)**; publish right before/during the trend. Also "shareable" long trends (memes) — use only if on-brand.
- **Seasonal** — publish a few weeks **before** the season so it's indexed in time.
- **General** — the bulk of posts; relevant for a while but dates within a year or two.

High-share / high-link formats: list posts, in-depth how-tos, interviews, checklists, case studies, white papers (B2B).

## Content marketing (Eric Enge's framework)
Three areas of focus: **build reputation**, **expand audience**, **grow visibility with media** → this is the best/safest way to earn high-quality links.

### Anchor content
- A single outstanding piece that drives big reputation/visibility gains. Must be **excellent, not just good**.
- Best types: **data-driven research studies** (most replicable), comprehensive guides, original interactives, expert interviews. Must be materially better than what exists, on a topic many care about, credible author/publisher.
- Takes time: programs can need **18–24 months** to reach critical mass. Keep the anchor content coming; once you have author credibility, even mundane content gets engagement.

### Market data on shares vs. links (Moz + BuzzSumo, ~2014)
- **75%** of content gets ≤ 39 shares; most gets little attention.
- **Social shares and links are NOT correlated** (~0.011 across 100k posts; no network correlated strongly). Don't use shares as your core metric.
- What *does* earn both shares + links: **opinion-forming journalism** (needs big reputation) and **data-driven research studies** (executable by most).
- **Longer-form content** tends to attract more links (when well done, on the right topic). Most articles are < 1,000 words; few exceed 3,000.

### Building comprehensive coverage
For a topic area: (1) comprehensive keyword research; (2) pull competitor ranking reports; (3) identify competitor pages ranking for those terms; (4) mine internal knowledge + on-site search; (5) build a content plan **more comprehensive than competitors**, including very specific low/no-volume questions. Use real **subject-matter experts (SMEs)** for quality.

### Skyscraper technique
1. Pick a topic coverable in one piece. 2. Find content ranking top-3 that can be beaten (e.g., outdated definition pages). 3. Pull backlink reports of those pages. 4. Outline a superior, comprehensive piece. 5. SME drives quality. 6. Publish + outreach to sites linking to the inferior content. (Classic example: updating outdated SEO stats, e.g., "YouTube is the #2 search engine" — then reaching out for links.)

### Partnerships
Co-create with other organizations / data providers (Backlinko × Semrush study). Both sides bring value; a third-party analysis adds objectivity/credibility. Validate market demand for the data before producing it.

### POEM channels
**P**aid, **O**wned, **E**arned **M**edia + social. You can use paid media for **exposure** (not for buying link value). Treat the market as an **ecosystem/community**; add value, build relationships with media/bloggers/influencers/audience.

### Pre-pitching under embargo
For a major data piece, offer top-tier media advance access "under embargo" (they don't publish before your go-live), then publish simultaneously and roll out wider promotion.
