# 01 — AI Search: AI Overviews & AI Mode (2025–2026)

## The shift
The biggest change since the course: Google search results are increasingly **AI-generated answers**, not just 10 blue links.
- **AI Overviews (AIO)** — AI-generated summaries at the top of results that synthesize multiple sources and cite some of them. Appear on roughly **4.5–12.5%** of queries (growing, varies by study/vertical).
- **AI Mode** — a fuller conversational/generative search experience (expanded through Google I/O 2025–2026), shifting users from "search → click" to "ask → get answer in place."

## Impact on traffic (the core problem)
- Queries that trigger an AI Overview see a **~30–50% reduction in organic CTR** for the top 3 positions vs. queries without one (Semrush, Ahrefs, independent studies).
- **Zero-click searches** keep rising — many queries are answered without any click (a trend Rand Fishkin/SparkToro flagged years ago, now amplified by AIO).
- **New visibility currency:** being **cited inside the AI Overview**. Brands cited in AIO reportedly earn **~35% more** organic clicks than non-cited competitors. Ranking #1 no longer guarantees the click.

## What this means for strategy
1. **Optimize to be a cited source**, not just to rank (see `02_geo-aeo-optimization.md`).
2. Expect **fewer clicks per impression** on informational queries → focus value on:
   - **Mid/bottom-funnel, transactional, and branded** queries where clicks still convert.
   - **Comprehensive, original, experience-rich** content AIO must cite (it can't synthesize what doesn't exist).
3. **Measure differently** — track AIO presence/citation, branded search lift, and conversions, not just rank + raw organic traffic (see `07_measurement-2026.md`).
4. The **fundamentals still hold**: relevance, comprehensiveness, E-E-A-T, technical crawlability, links/authority — AIO selects from the same high-quality, authoritative, well-structured content. It just changes *where* visibility happens.

## Continuity with the course
This is the natural continuation of two themes the course already taught: (a) Google moving toward **answering intent directly** (featured snippets → "position zero" → AIO), and (b) defending quality against low-cost AI content. The course's featured-snippet/zero-click discussion is the seed; AIO is the full tree.
